AI-Enabled Licensing Assistant · Prototype Overview

An executive and stakeholder summary of the Phase 1 prototype — what it is, who it serves, how it is structured, and what decisions are needed to move forward.

Last updated: August 11, 2026  ·  Phase 1 prototype — Build complete  ·  Child care + adult day care licensing  ·  Internal staff pilot

Executive Summary

What this prototype is

This is a 24-hour rapid prototype of an AI-enabled intake and triage assistant for Minnesota state licensing staff. It covers child care and adult day care licensing — two license types selected to prove the concept before scaling.

All data in the prototype is mocked. There are no real AI calls, no authentication, and no live integrations. The prototype exists to demonstrate the end-to-end workflow for internal staff and to surface design and product decisions before committing to a production build.

The prototype is built in React + Vite + TypeScript and is deployable to Vercel for review. It is designed to be portable — components can later be re-implemented in Salesforce Lightning (SLDS 2) as the production platform.

Why it exists

Providers today navigate fragmented, duplicative licensing processes that vary by county and tribe. Staff spend significant time on manual intake, triage, and data reconciliation. Anticipated legislation will consolidate these processes, and this project establishes the AI-enabled front door that makes that consolidation possible.

Phase 1 focuses on internal staff only. Licensing Intake Specialists, Provider Enrollment Specialists, and Program Managers are the primary users. A future phase will extend the assistant to providers directly.

The prototype answers the question: "Can an AI assistant meaningfully reduce triage time and improve data quality for MN licensing staff?"

Definition of done for Phase 1: A staff user can enter intake data, run the assistant, see a suggested license type + routing with explanation, accept or override the suggestion, view completeness checks with missing-item warnings, detect potential duplicate providers, ask a natural language question in a chat panel, and view a full interaction log — all in a single session.

Prototype Goals & Success Criteria

Core goals

  1. Create a unified experience. Reduce fragmentation across licensing, certification, and subsidy processes.
  2. Accessible, usable information. Clear, consistent guidance for staff, managers, and leadership.
  3. Eliminate duplication of work. Streamline manual intake and triage tasks for licensing staff.
  4. Centralize intake. Establish a common front door for provider-related licensing requests.
  5. Improve data integrity. Flag inconsistencies and duplicates at intake time, before they reach downstream systems.

Prototype success criteria

  • Staff can classify and route an intake in fewer steps than today's manual process.
  • Completeness checks surface missing fields and documents without staff needing to memorize requirements.
  • The duplicate-detection warning reduces misrouted or re-keyed cases.
  • Staff can get a plain-language answer to a policy question from the chat panel without leaving the tool.
  • Every assistant suggestion is logged — accept or override — for accountability and future model training.
  • The UI meets WCAG 2.1 AA contrast and keyboard navigation standards.

Strategic Alignment

How this fits the broader initiative

This prototype is the first step in a multi-phase program to deliver a unified provider experience across licensing, certification, and subsidy. It is designed to sit on top of existing integration layers — Apigee and custom APIs — rather than replace them.

The prototype is explicitly built for portability. Business logic lives in mock rule files (mockRules.ts) that can be replaced with live API calls. UI components are designed to map 1:1 to Salesforce Lightning Web Components when the production platform is ready. No county or tribal process rules are hardcoded in a way that prevents future policy updates.

Anticipated legislation alignment

Upcoming legislation aims to consolidate licensing processes across counties and tribes. The assistant is designed to be policy-aware and updatable — rules live in a single configuration file, not scattered through the UI. This makes the tool adaptable to legislative changes without a full rebuild.

Provider trust considerations

The assistant is assistive only — it never decides. Every suggestion includes an explanation ("why"). Staff can accept or override any recommendation. This design principle is essential for building trust with providers and staff who may be skeptical of AI-driven tools.

Primary Personas

Phase 1 serves internal staff only (personas 2–4). The provider-facing experience (persona 1) is defined now so design decisions are informed by the full picture, but it will be surfaced in a later phase.
External · Phase 2

Licensed Provider / Applicant

Child care centers, home-based providers, and other licensed organizations who must obtain and maintain licenses. Today their experience is fragmented, duplicative, and highly dependent on knowing "who to call." They struggle to understand which process applies, what documents are required, and where they are in the process.

Internal · Phase 1

Licensing Intake Specialist

Front-line staff who receive and review incoming applications via portals, email, and paper. They classify requests, check completeness, and route items to the appropriate team. They face high volume, inconsistent submissions, and complex rules that vary by county and tribe — leading to misrouted cases, rework, and delays.

Primary user of the intake form and assistant panel.

Internal · Phase 1

Provider Enrollment / Credentialing Specialist

Staff responsible for creating and maintaining provider records across licensing, certification, and subsidy programs. They reconcile information from multiple systems and manual sources. Their work is hampered by circular data flows, lack of a clear source of truth, and repeated manual data entry.

Primary user of completeness checks and duplicate detection.

Internal · Phase 1

Program Manager

Leaders who oversee licensing and subsidy program operations across regions or programs. Accountable for performance, compliance, and readiness for anticipated legislative changes. They need clear visibility into workload, bottlenecks, and data quality — but today must piece together information from disparate systems and reports.

Primary consumer of the interaction log and oversight dashboard.

Architecture & Screen Structure

The prototype is a single-page application — one screen, no routing. A staff user works through the full intake-to-log workflow without navigating away. This matches the way intake specialists currently work: one case at a time, linearly.

Screen layout

The screen is divided into two columns on desktop (stacked vertically on mobile). The left column holds the intake form. The right column holds all assistant panels, stacked in the order a user would engage with them during an intake session.

State management & data flow

State is managed in App.tsx and passed down as props. There is no external state library (no Redux, no Zustand) — the prototype is intentionally simple. The interaction log is a shared array in App.tsx; both AssistantPanel and StaffChatPanel append entries to it via an onLogEntry callback prop.

Left column (input)

  • IntakeForm owns its field state locally
  • On "Run Assistant": form data is lifted to App.tsx
  • App.tsx passes current intake data to all right-column panels

Right column (output)

  • AssistantPanel receives intake data, returns confirmed license type + routing
  • CompletenessPanel receives intake data + confirmed license type
  • StaffChatPanel is independent; logs via onLogEntry
  • LogPanel receives the shared log array; renders only

Accessibility standards

The prototype is built to WCAG 2.1 AA throughout. All form fields have associated <label> elements. All interactive elements are keyboard-reachable via Tab. Semantic HTML landmarks are used (<header>, <main>, <nav>). A "Skip to main content" link is present at the top of every page. No information is conveyed by color alone. Focus indicators are visible on all focusable elements. Minnesota brand colors are used only in combinations that meet AA contrast ratios.

Tech stack

  • Framework: Vite + React with TypeScript (--template react-ts)
  • Styling: Custom CSS using MN brand variables from theme.css; no component library in Phase 1
  • Data: All mock — no external API calls, no authentication, no database
  • Deployment: Vercel (build: npm run build, output: dist/)
  • Future platform: Salesforce Lightning (SLDS 2 / LWC) — out of scope for Phase 1

End-to-End Testing Guide

Each scenario below is self-contained — you can run them in any order. Refresh the page between scenarios to reset session state.

Scenario 1 — Child Care Classification, Accept

Persona: Licensing Intake Specialist  ·  Jobs: J-1, J-2, J-3, J-7

  1. In Provider Name, type Bright Futures Child Care.
  2. In Street Address, type 200 Oak Street.
  3. In City, type Minneapolis.
  4. In County, select Hennepin.
  5. Leave Application Type as New Application.
  6. In Program Description, type: Family child care home providing care for infants and toddlers.
  7. Click Run Assistant.

Expected results

  • AssistantPanel shows suggested license type: Child Care (DCYF) with an explanation citing "child," "infants," or "toddlers" as matching terms.
  • Routing suggestion shows: Child Care Licensing — DCYF, Hennepin County.
  • Click Accept Suggestion.
  • CompletenessPanel appears showing required fields (all present) and required documents (all unchecked = missing). Status badge reads "3 items missing — cannot mark ready."
  • Suggested next actions appear: "Request background study form," "Request floor plan," "Request first aid/CPR certificate."
  • LogPanel gains two new entries: one Classification (Accepted) and one Routing (Accepted).

Scenario 2 — Adult Day Care Classification, Override

Persona: Licensing Intake Specialist  ·  Jobs: J-1, J-2, J-7

  1. In Provider Name, type Sunrise Day Program.
  2. In County, select Ramsey.
  3. In Program Description, type: Adult day services program for seniors with memory care needs.
  4. Click Run Assistant.
  5. Confirm the suggestion shows Adult Day Care (DHS).
  6. In the Override license type dropdown, select Child Care (DCYF).
  7. Click Override.

Expected results

  • AssistantPanel confirms Child Care (DCYF) as the staff-selected type, despite the assistant suggesting Adult Day Care.
  • LogPanel shows Classification entry with Overridden badge and both the suggested type and the override type in the suggestion summary.
  • Click Change classification to return to the pending state and re-accept the original suggestion if desired.

Scenario 3 — Duplicate Provider Detection

Persona: Provider Enrollment / Credentialing Specialist  ·  Jobs: J-4

  1. In Provider Name, type Sunrise Child Care.
  2. In Street Address, type 123 Main St.
  3. In County, select Hennepin.
  4. In Program Description, type: Child care center serving school-age children.
  5. Click Run Assistant, then click Accept Suggestion.

Expected results

  • CompletenessPanel displays a yellow warning box: "Potential duplicate detected. This may match Sunrise Child Care at 123 Main St, Hennepin County."
  • The warning has role="alert" — screen readers announce it immediately.
  • The application cannot be marked "Ready for review" until staff resolve the duplicate question.

Also try: Enter Evergreen Adult Day Care at 456 Oak Ave — triggers the adult day care duplicate record.

Scenario 4 — Completeness Check with Documents

Persona: Provider Enrollment / Credentialing Specialist  ·  Jobs: J-3

  1. Repeat the Scenario 1 intake setup (Bright Futures Child Care, Hennepin, new family child care description).
  2. Check Background study form and Floor plan in the Documents on File section.
  3. Click Run Assistant → Accept Suggestion.

Expected results

  • CompletenessPanel shows Background study form (✓) and Floor plan (✓) as present.
  • First aid / CPR certificate remains missing (!).
  • Status badge reads "1 item missing."
  • Now also check First aid / CPR certificate — the completeness panel updates in real time as intake fields change.
  • With all three checked: status badge changes to Ready for review (green).

Scenario 5 — Staff Chat Panel, Known Question

Persona: All internal staff  ·  Jobs: J-5, J-6, J-7

  1. In the Staff Assistant Chat panel, click the chip: What are the requirements for a new family child care license?

Expected results

  • The question appears as a staff bubble (right-aligned, MN Blue background).
  • The assistant responds with the full requirements list (application, background study, home inspection, first aid/CPR, floor plan).
  • Source reference appears below the response: Child Care Licensing Policy Manual, Section 3.2.
  • LogPanel gains a new Chat entry with the question as the input summary and the response ID (childcare-new-requirements) as the suggestion.

Also try typing: "How many children" → capacity limits response. "Background check" → background study response. "Who can I approve?" → disclaimer response (legal/eligibility keywords). "pizza" → fallback response.

Scenario 6 — Keyboard-Only Navigation

Persona: Any staff user with accessibility needs  ·  Requirement: WCAG 2.1 AA keyboard access

  1. Press Tab from the browser address bar — the first focus should land on the Skip to main content link. Press Enter to jump past the header.
  2. Tab through all form fields in order: Provider Name → Street → City → County → Application Type → Program Description → document checkboxes → Run Assistant button.
  3. Tab into the AssistantPanel controls: Accept Suggestion button, Override dropdown, Override button.
  4. Tab into the chat panel: suggested question chips (each focusable), chat input, Send button.
  5. Throughout: verify a visible focus indicator (green outline) is present on every focused element.

Expected results

  • Every interactive element is reachable by Tab alone — no mouse required.
  • Enter key submits chat input without clicking Send.
  • No focus traps (Tab always moves forward; Shift+Tab always moves back).
  • All form labels are read correctly by VoiceOver / NVDA when a field receives focus.

Assumptions

These assumptions underpin the current design and build plan. If any prove false, scope or approach may need to change.

  • Single staff user. The prototype assumes a single, logged-in staff user. Multi-user scenarios, role-based access, and permission levels are not addressed in Phase 1.
  • Two license types only. Child care and adult day care licensing are the scope for Phase 1. Other program areas (e.g., foster care, adult foster care) are explicitly out of scope.
  • Mocked AI behavior is sufficient for prototype validation. Keyword-based heuristics are sufficient to demonstrate the classification and routing flow. A real LLM or ML model is not required to validate the workflow with staff users.
  • All data is fictitious. No real provider names, addresses, or case data appear in the prototype. Mock data is representative of the shape and volume of real data, but not real data itself.
  • Session-only logging. The interaction log resets on page refresh. Persistent logging across sessions requires a backend — out of scope for Phase 1.
  • Desktop-first layout. The primary use case is a desktop workstation in a state office. Mobile layout is supported (responsive CSS) but is not the primary target for Phase 1.
  • React + Vite is acceptable for the prototype. The production platform will be Salesforce Lightning. The prototype uses React + Vite as a faster prototyping environment, with components designed to port to LWC.
